The GoalPre guide to reading football predictions

A plain-English walkthrough of probabilities, odds, models and value.

Football predictions can look like a wall of percentages and decimals. This guide explains, in everyday language, exactly what those numbers mean, how GoalPre calculates them, and — most importantly — how to read them sensibly. Everything here describes probabilities, not certainties. The aim is understanding, not a shortcut to guaranteed winnings, which do not exist.

1. The markets we cover

GoalPre focuses on the two markets most football fans understand instinctively:

  • 1X2 (match result). Three outcomes: 1 home win, X draw, 2 away win. The three probabilities always add up to 100%.
  • Over/Under 2.5 goals. Will the two teams combine for three or more goals (Over) or two or fewer (Under)? These two probabilities also add up to 100%.

We also show the single most likely correct score and the expected goals for each team — the average number of goals the model thinks each side will score.

2. What a probability really tells you

If GoalPre says a home win is 60% likely, it does not mean the home team will win. It means that, across many matches with these same underlying numbers, the home side would be expected to win roughly six times in ten. The other four times, something else happens — and that is completely normal, not a “wrong” prediction.

A 70% favourite still loses about three times in ten. A good model is one whose 70% picks win close to 70% of the time over a long run — not one that “never misses”, because no honest model can.

3. How the Poisson model works

The Poisson model is the classic mathematical approach to football and the backbone of GoalPre. In four steps:

  1. Rate each team. Every team gets an attack and a defence rating from the goals it scores and concedes, adjusted for the league average.
  2. Estimate expected goals. For a fixture we compute expected goals per side: roughly λ_home = attack_home × defence_away × league_avg × home_advantage, and the mirror image for the away team.
  3. Build a score grid. A Poisson distribution turns each expected-goals figure into the chance of 0, 1, 2, 3… goals. Combining the two sides gives the probability of every scoreline (0–0, 1–0, 2–1…).
  4. Add it up. Summing the grid produces the 1X2 probabilities, the most likely score and the Over/Under split. A Dixon-Coles correction nudges the low-scoring scorelines to match how real football behaves.

4. How the KPI model works

The KPI (Key Performance Indicator) model takes a different angle. Instead of goals alone, it builds weighted confidence indices from recent, venue-aware match statistics. It uses two separate frameworks whose weights each add up to 100%: one for the match winner (1X2) and one for the Over 2.5 goals line.

Threshold-style indicators (such as “AGF above 1.80”) are applied as smooth ramps rather than hard yes/no cut-offs, so a team just below a line is not treated the same as one far below it. For the goals line, leaky defences (high goals-against) raise the Over-2.5 score, because teams that concede freely tend to play in higher-scoring games.

Because it weights venue and form heavily, the KPI model sometimes disagrees with the pure Poisson model — and that disagreement is itself useful information (see confidence below).

5. How the AI-consensus model works

The AI engine does not replace the other two — it combines them. It blends the Poisson model (40%), the KPI model (35%) and the market’s own de-margined odds (25%) into a single consensus probability, then measures the gap between that consensus and the bookmaker price to flag possible value. It also writes a short, neutral, plain-English summary of the match. All of this is an estimate, not advice.

6. Odds, implied probability and the margin

Decimal odds and probability are two sides of the same coin: implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds. Odds of 2.00 imply a 50% chance; odds of 1.50 imply about 67%.

If you add up the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a real market, they come to more than 100%. That extra slice is the bookmaker’s margin (or “overround”) — their built-in profit. “De-margining” simply removes that slice so you can compare the market’s true opinion with a model on a like-for-like basis.

7. What “value” means

Value has a precise meaning: it is when your estimated probability is higher than the probability implied by the odds. If a model rates a team at 55% but the odds imply only 45%, that is a positive edge. GoalPre marks these with a ★ value flag.

Value is about the long run, not any single match. A value pick can still lose; an over-priced pick can still win. The point is which side of the price you are on across hundreds of bets — and even then, nothing is guaranteed.

8. Reading the confidence signal

On the AI engine, each match shows a confidence level (High / Medium / Low). It is built from two things: how clear the favourite is, and how much the Poisson and KPI models agree. When both models point the same way, confidence rises. When they disagree, treat the pick as lower conviction — the models are telling you the game is genuinely uncertain.

9. Five common mistakes

  1. Treating a probability as a certainty. 65% is not “will win”.
  2. Chasing short odds blindly. A low price is not a safe price.
  3. Ignoring the margin. Raw odds overstate the true chances.
  4. Judging a model on one result. Only the long run matters.
  5. Betting more than you can afford. Never do this — see below.

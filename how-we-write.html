How we write our match analysis

Our editorial process, in plain English — and a full worked example from data to finished article.

GoalPre's predictions are produced by statistical models, but our written match analysis is produced by people. Every article is researched from recent data, written and edited by a named human, and fact-checked before it goes live. We do not auto-publish machine-written text. This page explains how a piece is made and walks through one example step by step.

1. Our editorial principles

A few rules govern everything we publish:

  • Data first. No article starts until the recent statistics are gathered — expected goals (xG), shot rates, Over/Under 2.5 trends, form and head-to-head records.
  • A human always decides. Software can help us organise data, but a named writer makes the judgement calls and a second person reviews the piece before it is published.
  • We write few, not many. We cover a handful of genuinely interesting fixtures each week rather than churning out a page for every match. Quality over quantity.
  • Honesty over hype. We say when the model and the bookmakers simply agree and there is no edge, we give a confidence level, and we never claim a “sure thing” — because none exists.
  • Information, not advice. Everything is for study and entertainment. It is not betting advice, and the models cannot see injuries, motivation or weather.

2. The three-step process

Every article follows the same route from numbers to words:

  1. Build a data brief. We pull the recent stats for both teams into an internal brief — the factual backbone of the article.
  2. Add the human layer. The writer adds what the data alone cannot show: team news, fatigue from fixture congestion, how a side actually plays, and where our model disagrees with the market.
  3. Draft, edit and sign off. The piece is written in a human voice, every stat is verified, generic filler is cut, and a second person reviews and approves it before publishing.

Step 2 — the human layer

These are the notes the writer adds that the numbers alone miss:

  • Newcastle are on a short turnaround after European football — fatigue risk late in an away game.
  • Arsenal's main striker is a late fitness test; if he sits, the attacking projection and the Over lean soften.
  • Newcastle defend deep and counter on the road, which keeps games quiet for an hour before they leak late when chasing.
  • Model and market agree on the home win (no value there); on Over 2.5 the model is only a shade above the implied chance — a slim edge, not a signal.

Step 3 — the finished, published article

Arsenal vs Newcastle: control at home, but the goals line is a coin-flip

By the GoalPre analysis desk · illustrative date

Arsenal go into this one as clear favourites, and the underlying numbers back that up rather than just the league table. Over their last six games they have averaged 2.05 expected goals while conceding just 0.82 — the profile of a side creating a lot and giving up very little. At the Emirates, where they take 2.6 points per game, that advantage only grows.

Newcastle are the more awkward side to read. Four of their last six have gone over 2.5 goals, which looks tempting, but that number hides something. On the road they average barely over a point a game and tend to sit deep and counter — a plan that can keep the score down for an hour and then leak late once they are forced to chase. The trip also comes on a short turnaround after European football, and tired legs in the final 20 minutes are exactly when Arsenal punish teams at home.

Our model lands on a 58% home win, an expected total of around 2.9 goals, and Over 2.5 a shade above even money at 54%. That home figure lines up with the market price near 1.70, so there is no hidden value there — the bookmakers and the model simply agree. The goals line is the interesting part: the model is only marginally above the implied chance, which to us is a coin-flip, not a signal.

One thing to watch before kick-off is Arsenal's main striker, who is a late fitness call. If he starts, the attacking projection holds and the Over looks a little more live. If he is rested or unavailable, expect a more controlled, lower-scoring home win.

Our read: the home win is the confident call here. On goals we would lean Over only cautiously, and only if the first-choice forward line starts — otherwise leave the line alone. Confidence: high on the result, low on the total.

4. What we check before publishing

Before any article goes live, a reviewer confirms that:

  • Every statistic was pulled from a real source and is accurate — we never invent or “round into” a nicer story.
  • The piece contains specific, checkable detail and a genuine opinion with a confidence level, not generic filler.
  • It states honestly where there is no value, and makes no guarantees.
  • It carries an author byline, a date, and a responsible-gambling notice.

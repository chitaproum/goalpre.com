Reading a prediction: turning model output into a sensible decision

By GoalPre analysis desk · 26 October 2026 · 6 min read

A model gives you probabilities, not instructions. The skill is in reading them: separating confidence from value, and knowing that “no bet” is a perfectly good answer.

A probability is a weather forecast, not a promise

When our model says a home win is 58% likely, it is not predicting a home win. It is saying that in a long run of near-identical matches, the home side would win about 58 times in 100 — and lose or draw the other 42. A 58% pick that loses is not a wrong forecast any more than a 30%-chance-of-rain day that turns wet. Judge the process over many games, not one result.

Confidence versus value: two different questions

These get confused constantly, so we keep them separate.

  • Confidence is how likely the outcome is. A 72% favourite is a high-confidence outcome.
  • Value is whether the price is generous relative to that chance. A 72% shot at a price that implies 80% is high-confidence but poor value — you are being underpaid for the risk.

To check value, turn the odds into an implied probability: divide 1 by the decimal odds. Odds of 2.00 imply 50%; 1.50 imply 67%. If the model’s chance is meaningfully higher than the price’s implied chance, there may be an edge. If it is lower or the same, there is not — however confident the pick looks.

Worked example: model says 60%, odds are 2.10 (implied 47.6%). The model rates the outcome more likely than the price does — that is where value lives. Flip it: model 60%, odds 1.50 (implied 67%). Now the market rates it more likely than the model does — no value, pass.

When the right call is no call

Most matches are priced efficiently, so most of the time the sensible decision is to pass. That is not a failure of the model — it is the model doing its job, telling you there is nothing to beat.
